About the project

Bridging botanical heritage & modern drug discovery.

A cheminformatic analysis of secondary metabolites in Jordanian medicinal plants and their therapeutic potential — positioning Jordan’s flora between global natural-product libraries and approved drugs.

Background

Jordan possesses a rich, longstanding heritage of using natural resources for medicine. At the crossroads of Asia, Africa and Europe, and with topography ranging from Mediterranean highlands to arid desert, it hosts more than 2,500 plant species — many with recognized medicinal value, yet most uncharacterized computationally.

This study compiled 7,866 phytochemicals from 475 species, computed their physicochemical and complexity profiles, mapped their chemical space, and predicted biological targets — benchmarking throughout against COCONUT, LANaPDB and FDA-approved drugs. The result is both a resource and a method for prioritising natural-product drug-discovery candidates.

The work is fully open and reproducible, with the dataset and analyses deposited publicly.

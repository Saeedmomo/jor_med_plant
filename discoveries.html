Case study: repurposing a β-carboline

How network pharmacology took Harmol — an alkaloid from the desert plant Peganum harmala — from a known sedative chemistry to a candidate kinase modulator.

A

Start from the plant

Peganum harmala (Syrian rue / harmal) is widely used in Jordanian folk medicine and is rich in β-carboline alkaloids, including harmol and harmine.

B

Fingerprint & compare

ECFP4 fingerprints of harmol were compared against ChEMBL Actives. The strongest matches pointed to the kinase DYRK1A — a well-validated harmine target (IC₅₀ ≈ 33 nM).

C

Read the network

In the compound–target–pathway network, harmol additionally connected to HASPIN, a mitotic histone kinase — an association not previously reported for β-carbolines.

D

Generate the hypothesis

The HASPIN–β-carboline link, surfaced computationally for the first time here, positions harmol as a candidate modulator of cell-cycle and neurodevelopmental kinases — a testable lead for oncology and neuropharmacology.

The compound–target–pathway network

Built in Cytoscape, the network integrates ligand-based predictions with KEGG pathway enrichment, revealing how single phytochemicals reach across many biological processes.

The network exposes mechanisms spanning glycoprotein processing (castanospermine → mannosidases), kinase regulation (β-carbolines → DYRK / HASPIN), immune modulation (microbial metabolites → KLRB1A / CD161) and protease inhibition (polyphenols → SUB1).

Many compounds behave as multi-target hubs — the structural basis of natural products’ characteristic polypharmacology, and a rich seam for repurposing.

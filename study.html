The chemistry & healing heritage of Jordan’s medicinal flora.

A computational atlas of 7,866 secondary metabolites from 475 Jordanian medicinal plants — paired with a bilingual (Arabic / English) ethnobotanical catalog of parts used, traditional uses and chemical constituents — mapping their chemical space, structural diversity and predicted therapeutic targets to accelerate natural-product drug discovery.

A research portal, not a webpage.

Jordan hosts over 2,500 plant species at the crossroads of Asia, Africa and Europe — yet most of their chemistry has never been mapped computationally.

This project compiles a curated phytochemical dataset through extensive literature mining and benchmarks it against the global natural-product collections COCONUT and LANaPDB, as well as FDA-approved drugs. Using RDKit we compute molecular descriptors, Bemis–Murcko scaffolds and a normalized spacial-score (nSPS) complexity metric, then visualise chemical space with PCA, t-SNE and UMAP.

Ligand-based target prediction against ChEMBL Actives, KEGG pathway enrichment, and a compound–target–pathway network built in Cytoscape reveal both established and novel polypharmacological associations — spanning glycoprotein processing, kinase regulation, immune modulation and protease inhibition.

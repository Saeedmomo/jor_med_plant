The scientific approach

Methods & AI.

A reproducible, in-silico pipeline turns a list of plant chemicals into testable biological hypotheses — characterizing chemical space, predicting protein targets, and prioritising candidates for the lab.

⚛

Cheminformatics

RDKit descriptors, Bemis–Murcko scaffolds and nSPS complexity to profile structure and drug-likeness.

🕸

Network pharmacology

Compound–target–pathway networks in Cytoscape to read systems-level mechanisms.

🧩

Molecular docking

Structure-based scoring to test predicted ligand–target binding poses for prioritised pairs.

🔍

Virtual screening

Fingerprint similarity against ChEMBL Actives to flag the most promising metabolites at scale.

🧠

Machine learning

Similarity- and model-based inference linking phytochemicals to bioactivity classes.

🔄

Drug repurposing

Mapping natural products onto validated targets to reveal new uses for known chemistry.

🌿

NP discovery

Surfacing novel chemotypes and untapped species for experimental follow-up.

The pipeline, step by step

1

Curate & standardize

Phytochemicals mined from Google Scholar, Scopus and PubMed are cleaned with RDKit — salt stripping, tautomer normalization and protonation correction — then deduplicated.

2

Compute descriptors

Molecular weight, SlogP, TPSA, H-bond donors/acceptors and rotatable bonds, benchmarked against COCONUT, LANaPDB and FDA drugs.

3

Map chemical space

Morgan / ECFP4 fingerprints reduced with PCA, t-SNE and UMAP to reveal clustering and novel chemotypes.

4

Predict targets

Tanimoto similarity to ChEMBL Actives (IC₅₀ < 1000 nM, confidence ≥ 7) infers the most likely protein targets per compound.

5

Enrich pathways & build the network

Target genes are enriched against KEGG via Enrichr, and a compound–target–pathway network is assembled in Cytoscape for interpretation.

Tools: RDKit · Cytoscape 3.10.3 · Enrichr / KEGG 2021 · ECFP4 fingerprints · Tanimoto ≥ 0.6 · ChEMBL (365,330 interactions, 3,363 targets).
